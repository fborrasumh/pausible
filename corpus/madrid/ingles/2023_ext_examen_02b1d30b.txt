                                               TEXTO A

                                  Coins Treasure Hidden in Kitchen

  While renovating their kitchen in July 2019, residents discovered a cup hidden underneath the
  concrete and floorboards of their home, according to the auction house Spink & Son press release
  sent to CNN. The cup, described as being no larger than a soft-drink can, contained over 260 gold
  coins dating from 1610 to 1727. The coins have an estimated value of £100,000 in today's spending
  power and could be easily sold to collectors for £250,000.

  Gregory Edmund, from Spink & Son, said the remarkable treasure is unlike any find in British
  archaeology or any coin auction in living memory. “It is a wonderful and truly unexpected discovery
  from such a modest location. This coin treasure is also one of the largest on archaeological record
  from Britain, and definitely for the 18th century period.”

  “The coins almost certainly belonged to Joseph and Sarah Maisters, who married in 1694”, reads
  the press release. According to Spink & Son, the Maisters were an influential mercantile family
  from the 16th century to the 18th century. They traded iron, timber and coal from the Baltic states
  and several generations took up posts as lawmakers in the early 1700s. Their family line
  disappeared soon after the couple died, and the coins were never retrieved.

  Edmund said the findings reflect the standard £50 and £100 coins that were used at the time of the
  English Civil War. Joseph and Sarah chose to keep the money at home because they clearly
  distrusted the newly-formed Bank of England, the “banknote” and even the gold coinage of their
  day. Why nobody ever retrieved the coins when they were really easy to find just beneath the
  original 18th century floorboards is an even bigger mystery.




  Adapted from “Coins worth up to $290,000 found under kitchen floorboards,” CNN, September 2,
  2022. <https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/gold-coins-england-kitchen-intl-scli/index.html>

                        The "4-Color" BIC©: A Timeless 50-Year-Old Pen!

  Its smooth design has never changed. Emblematic, unique and original, the plastic Bic© 4-color
  pen has left its mark on the world over several generations. In 2020, this timeless cult object
  celebrated its 50th anniversary and the good news is that it hasn't aged a day! The pen was born
  within the Bic company in 1969, based on an idea developed by its founder, Marcel Bich. He wanted
  to create an item that made life easier for consumers by making it compact, resistant, reliable and
  affordable. It soon became a worldwide success and was first marketed in the United States in
  1971.

  Very light and fun with its characteristic click, this apparently simple pen is in fact very complex, a
  real concentration of technology. It has very precise machinery that allows the desired color to be
  selected mechanically, very innovative plastics that can resist many activations and high-quality
  ink. It is appreciated as much for its look, its simplicity, and its unique functionality as for its
  resistance!

  The 4-color pen originally came in 2 versions: one with an orange body and a fine tip, the favorite
  of schoolchildren and students alike, the other with a blue body and a medium tip. Since then, the
  range has expanded and there are now around thirty varieties of 4-color Bic pens, including the "4
  Fluorescent Colors" Bic launched in 2016, where green is replaced by fluorescent yellow ink, and
  the 2017 version comprising an integrated mechanical pencil instead of the green ink.

  One thing is certain: the manufacturing process of the "4-color" pen has remained the same for 50
  years. Classified as an industrial secret, its technology is jealously guarded, and few people have
  been able to enter the factory where it has been manufactured since its beginnings.




  Adapted from “The "4-colour" BIC©: a timeless 50-year-old pen!” PlasticsleMag, 17 February 2021.
  <https://plastics-themag.com/The-4-colour-BIC:-a-timeless-50-year-old-pen>
