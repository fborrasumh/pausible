                                     A Park Bench with an Address

   In an unusual move that highlights British attention to detail, a park bench in Portland Square,
   Bristol, has been officially given an address—complete with its own postcode. This is not a silly
   gesture done by children, but a practical solution initiated by Bristol’s local health authority. The
   decision was made to help six homeless individuals who regularly sleep on the bench to have
   access to free medical care.

   In Britain, registering for healthcare typically requires a fixed address, which many homeless people
   lack. By assigning an address to the bench, the health authority created a way for these individuals
   to qualify for treatment. As reported in The Indian Express, this act of compassion ensures that
   people who sleep outside are not excluded from essential services. Tony Palmer, manager of a
   local clinic that looks after many homeless patients, supports the initiative. “Homeless people are
   never turned away,” he explains, “but the invented address allows us to give rough sleepers better
   care.” While the bench is centrally located, it is not exactly a desirable residence. Still, public
   interest in the bench has grown since the announcement.

   Homelessness remains a serious issue in Britain. Many people live on the streets, surviving with
   few possessions and asking for money or looking for food in trash. London, despite its wealth and
   glamour, has the highest concentration of homeless individuals in the country. The problem has
   even caused cultural responses—pop singer Phil Collins once wrote Another Day in Paradise to
   draw attention to the difficult situation of the homeless in London.

   This situation is particularly troubling given that Britain is a welfare state. The government is
   expected to provide basic necessities such as free healthcare, education and housing. Yet the
   presence of so many homeless people suggests that the system is not working as effectively as it
   should. Still, the gesture of giving a park bench an address is a touching example of local authorities
   trying to make a difference. It shows a desire to adapt the system to meet real human needs.
   Perhaps this small act will inspire similar efforts in other countries, reminding us that even modest
   changes can have a meaningful impact.

La valoración y los objetivos de cada una de estas preguntas son los siguientes:

Pregunta 1: Hasta 2 puntos. Se trata de medir exclusivamente la comprensión lectora. Se
ofrecen tres enunciados y el estudiante responde sobre dos de ellos, a su elección, decidiendo

given”, NG). En los casos de verdadero o falso se deberá justificar la respuesta citando el
fragmento del texto correspondiente. Si la información no se facilita en el texto (es decir, si la
respuesta es “Not given”, NG), el estudiante no tendrá que escribir nada más. Se otorgará 1
punto por cada apartado. Se calificará con 0 puntos la opción elegida de verdadero o falso
que no vaya justificada o si se aporta alguna cita del texto cuando la respuesta sea “Not given”.

Pregunta 2: Hasta 2 puntos. Se pretende evaluar dos competencias fundamentalmente: la
comprensión lectora y la producción escrita, mediante la formulación de dos cuestiones de
respuesta abierta que el estudiante deberá contestar basándose en la información del texto.
La respuesta no puede ser copia literal del texto, aunque se puede emplear alguna palabra
que aparezca en el texto. Cada una de las preguntas valdrá 1 punto, asignándose 0,5 puntos
por las ideas reflejadas, siempre que estén parafraseadas, y 0,5 por la expresión de las
mismas.

apartado.

carácter unitario, siempre y cuando se construya una oración ortográfica, gramatical y
semánticamente correcta, de acuerdo con las indicaciones dadas.

Pregunta 5: Hasta 3 puntos. Se trata de la redacción de un texto sencillo de diversa tipología

los aspectos sobre los que trate el texto o con cualquier otro tema de relevancia personal para
el alumnado o de interés general.
