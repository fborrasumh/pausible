                                               “ALEXA, TAKE ME TO THE MOON”

      Lockheed Martin, which is building the Orion spacecraft that NASA hopes will fly astronauts to the
      moon within a couple of years, is partnering with Amazon to put an Alexa on the capsule.

      The device would give astronauts real-time information on telemetry, the health of the spacecraft
      and its speed. It would be possible “for astronauts to ask about the spacecraft, the mission, the
      subsystems,” Aaron Rubenson, Amazon’s vice president, said. “More importantly, from the crew
      perspective: What’s that alarm that just went off? Remind me what that is.”

      In addition to Alexa, the Orion spacecraft will be outfitted with screens that, like Zoom, allow users
      to see each other and share critical information. Taken together, the package, known as Callisto,
      would help translate different languages so astronauts from all over the world could communicate.
      It would also help controllers on the ground share information over a screen. The platform would
      also help in overcoming isolation by allowing astronauts to stay and feel connected to their home,
      family members and friends.

      The onboard space Alexa would not be connected to the Internet but instead connect directly to
      Orion’s computer and its own onboard cloud. Still, it could send queries back to Earth and connect
      with the cloud to retrieve all sorts of information, such as news, that would help astronauts to feel
      less lonely. There would be a lag in those queries since data would have to travel from the
      spacecraft to Earth and then from Earth to the spacecraft.


      Adapted from an article by Christian Davenport, The Guardian, 5-1-2022

                     2022: EUROPEAN YEAR OF YOUTH TO EMPOWER YOUNG PEOPLE

       The 2022 European Year of Youth (EYY) will focus on re-establishing a positive outlook for
       European young people negatively affected by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Designating
       2022 as a year focused on young people means increased efforts will be made to include youth
       priorities in relevant EU policy areas and at every level of the EU’s decision-making process.

       The EYY will aim to engage young people in key consultation processes, such as the Conference on
       the Future of Europe, and in other public policies at EU, national and local levels. There will be a
       special focus on young people with fewer opportunities and on mental health issues. The EYY will
       include conferences, initiatives promoting youth participation in policy making, awareness-raising
       campaigns on a more inclusive, green, and digital EU, as well as studies and research on the
       situation in which young people in the EU find themselves.

       “The COVID-19 pandemic continues to disproportionately affect young people”, said Sabine
       Verheyen. “They face the challenges of trying to continue their education and enter the job market
       (...) make new acquaintances and discover other countries. Young people have accepted and
       supported the ongoing restrictions out of solidarity. Now it is time to let them flourish”, she said.
       “This year will lay the foundation for giving young people a stronger platform to make their voices
       heard”, she stressed, adding that we need “all young people to be involved and participate in
       creating a better, greener, more digital and inclusive future in Europe.”


       Adapted from an article in EU Monitor, 15-12-2021
