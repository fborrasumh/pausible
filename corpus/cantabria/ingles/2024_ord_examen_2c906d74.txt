                   South African students using vaping to cope with exam stress


1        In South Africa, where academic success is highly valued, vaping (the use of electronic cigarettes) has
    gained popularity as a mechanism to deal with exam stress.
          While the intentions behind turning to vaping might initially seem harmless, it's essential to understand the
2
    potential risks and consequences associated with it. Vaping involves inhaling vaporized liquids containing
    nicotine and other chemicals, which can negatively affect various cognitive functions, such as attention, learning,
    mood, and impulse control.
3         During exams, vaping may seem attractive due to the misconception that it offers a quick stress relief
    solution and is healthier than cigarettes. Dr Catherine Egbe of the South African Medical Research Council said
    there’s no study available to prove the popular belief that vaping is safer than smoking cigarettes and highlights
    healthier alternatives for stress management, such as mindfulness, getting adequate sleep, and a balanced diet.
4         Diverse studies have found that nicotine also predisposes teen brains to other kinds of addictions,
    meaning they are at a higher risk of developing an addiction to alcohol and other drugs. Recognizing the
    severity of this problem, the country has tried to reduce vaping among teenagers. For instance, there are
    strict rules against selling vaping products to minors, aiming to keep these products away from young people.
    Additionally, there have been efforts to limit advertising about vaping in places where teenagers often see
    them. Deborah Arnott, chief executive of the charity Action on Smoking and Health, has expressed her concern
    about the promotion of e-cigarettes to children on social media platforms including TikTok.
    Furthermore, some schools have implemented stress-reduction programs, providing students with effective
    tools to cope with academic pressures without turning to harmful habits.
5         The economic implications are vast, with substantial costs for the nation's healthcare system. Nicotine
    increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases, such as hypertension, heart attack, reproductive disorders, and many
    types of cancers. This results in higher healthcare spending, as treating health issues linked to vaping requires a
    significant amount of funds. Hospitals must invest in specialized treatments and medical professionals to address
    the diverse health consequences of vaping. As a result, the overall cost of healthcare rises. Furthermore,
    when people become unwell, it affects their ability to work effectively, impacting the country's economic
    productivity. Additionally, as health issues related to vaping become more widespread, the demand for disability
    benefits and rehabilitation programs rises, putting an extra financial burden on the social support system of the
    country.
6
         Health experts are worried that e-cigarettes might lead young people to start smoking tobacco. A study
    conducted by the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) found that young e-cigarette users were three times
    more likely to start smoking cigarettes. The association Cancer Research calls for strict rules to make sure
    e-cigarettes are only used by smokers trying to quit and emphasizes that non-smokers should never use them.

                                 The song that has made Bad Bunny angry
1       Last week, the anonymous artist FlowGPT surprised reggaeton enthusiasts after publishing “Nostalgia”, a
  song created with Artificial Intelligence (AI) that imitates the voices of Bad Bunny, Daddy Yankee, and Justin
  Bieber, introducing what many consider a potential new favorite “perreo” anthem.
2          The AI-created masterpiece rapidly gained popularity, captivating thousands of social media users
  who shared videos enthusiastically mimicking the verses of the virtual stars. “Nostalgia” turned into a
  viral phenomenon, even reaching the ears of Bad Bunny himself. However, the artist's reaction was unexpected
  and far from positive, as revealed in messages shared within his WhatsApp group. “If you guys are vibing with
  that viral song on TikTok, leave this group immediately. You don’t deserve to be my friends”, he wrote.
3          Bad Bunny's remarks caused a division among his followers, particularly those who had welcomed
  “Nostalgia.” Some disagreed with the artist, suggesting that if people preferred an AI-generated song over his
  original work, it might signal a need for reflection. Others defended Bad Bunny, urging those who liked
  the AI-generated track to seek medical attention for their “earwax”.
4          The controversy deepened as even Bad Gyal, another prominent artist, shared a TikTok video using
  the AI-generated song. Although at the beginning she showed excitement, when she found that the song had
  been created with AI, she expressed her disappointment.
5          In response to the escalating dispute, the AI-generated track, “Nostalgia,” has been removed from digital
  platforms, making fans wonder what might happen next. The controversy about this AI-generated reggaeton song
  shows how AI is used more in the music world. People are using AI to try and make songs that sound like famous
  singers. But it's not just this. AI helps in different ways too. For example, when you listen to music on some
  apps, they use AI to suggest songs you might like based on what you've listened to before. Moreover, AI
  can help musicians come up with new song ideas, serving more as an assistant by suggesting melodies or
  mixing audio, rather than being the sole creator of the whole composition. Some artists are even teaming
  up with AI to create special performances. This involves musicians playing along with AI-generated sounds to
  make unique concert experiences. It's like a new way for artists to use technology in their shows.
6          The evolving intersection of technology and music raises questions about copyright, ownership, and
    artistic integrity. While some argue that AI can be a valuable tool for music creation, others vehemently reject
    the notion of cloning voices and artistic expression through artificial means and debate about whether music

    created by AI can be considered the intellectual property of the machine or its human collaborators.
