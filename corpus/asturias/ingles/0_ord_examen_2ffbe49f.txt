1. What does the expression ‘British people seem to be ridiculously keen on “fresh air”’ mean in the context
of the text dealing with British houses? Why is that?

It means that Britishers don’t dislike being cold at home. In fact, British houses are the coldest in Europe
because they are older than in any other country and they are poorly insulated, too.

2. What is the British idea of domestic comfort? Is it related to elegance?

The British idea of domestic comfort is limited to feeling cosy, in other words, to creating an atmosphere
which seems warm and which links with tradition. This is more important than elegance, which explains why
British people are generally considered to have bad taste.

3. Give examples of bad taste, as mentioned in the text.

Cheap, mass-produced furniture; chairs and sofas covered in synthetic material; filling up a room with
ornaments; an imitation open fire with plastic coal powered by electricity.

4. How many living rooms can you find in most older houses? For what reason?

In most older houses you can find two living rooms: one at the front of the house, for formal visits; and one
more living room at the back of the house, for family members and close friends. This second living room is
more private (hidden from public view) and is considered more comfortable (privacy is an element of
comfort).
