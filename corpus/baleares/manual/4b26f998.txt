Eva Mendes reveals she doesn´t let her daughters use the   i nternet

Eva Mendes   has recently revealed that she doesn’t let her and   Ryan Gosling ’s   daughters   use the

i nternet. The actress, 49, took   to Instagram   on 3 August to share one of the rules that she has for

her   and   Gosling’s   daughters — Esmeralda,   eight,   and   Amada,   seven.   Mendes   shared   a   video   of

herself shaking her head no and wagging her finger, as she pointed out that’s how she responds to

her children when they ask to use the   i nternet. In the caption, she wrote [this is what I do] “when

my kids ask me if they’re old enough to go on the   i nternet, social media or anything requiring wi -

fi”.

As would be expected, many people commented her post. One fan asked when she will

allow her children to use the   i nternet. Mendes candidly confessed that she’s still figuring that out.

She also specified that she’s been doing research on how social media can impact children as they

continue to get older. “I honestly don’t know. I have to see where it goes. For now, I’m observing

them and keeping up with research about how social media can impact a child’s brain”.

When   another   fan   praised   her   parenting   decision,   Mendes   noted   that   while   she’s   “just

sharing what [she] feels now”, she thinks it’ll be harder to keep her children away from the   i nternet

as they get older. She clarified that her children are still given screentime, adding: “I let my kids

use the iPad to make movies and watch films I’ve downloaded, but I don’t have   i nternet access on

the iPad”.

The actress was also hit with some criticism for her parenting rule, with one Instagram user

writing: “We are in the   i nternet century so eventually everyone will need or want to access the

i nternet”. This comment appears to have been deleted but before it was, Mendes got a chance to

explain why she doesn’t feel like the   i nternet is a safe space for her children. “In my house, children

do not have access to the   i nternet as it’s too dangerous. Just like drinking or voting or getting a

driver’s   license   (etc..)   isn’t   allowed   for   children,   the   i nternet   falls under that   category   for   me.

Especially social media”.

While Mendes and Gosling — who’ve been together since 2011 — are notoriously private about

their family life, the couple have shared a few details about raising children together. During an

interview with   Byrdie   in July 2022,   Mendes explained that she and her partner want to make sure

that their children feel confident in themselves throughout everything they do in life. “Hopefully

Ryan and I are doing the work by just loving them, and doing most of that work for them so that

they grow up feeling like they’re enough”, she said. “That’s the one thing that’s really important to

me. Because once they feel like they’re enough, no matter what they do, no matter what they end

up doing, that will contribute to every area of your life”.

Mysterious   masked   guards   in   pink   boiler   suits,   a   singing   giant   doll ,   and   a   life -

changing   sum   of   money...   This   can   only   mean   the   return   of   Squid   Game

The Netflix hit has come back to our screens, but this time with a reality game show spin - off called

Squid Game: The Challenge . Over 450 real players battle it out for a $4.56m (£3.66m) prize, one of

the biggest cash prizes in TV history.

The show kicks off with hundreds of contestants in green tracksuits trying to cross a line

without being seen. Their mission is to get past a 4.2m doll   that is   furiously spinning her neck. In

Red   Light, Green   Light, radio - controlled exploding dye   takes out   the   players who   have   tripped

over — instead of a fatal bullet as in the fictional version. "It felt like it was real, it didn't feel like you

were in a fictional place", Lorenzo Nobilio told BBC News. He described it as the physically hardest

game on the show: "I made it past the line in seven hours, that was a very long time, but it's called

Squid Game: The Challenge , it's not an all - inclusive holiday in the Canary Islands".

The   show   made   headlines   earlier   this   year   because   some   of   the   contestants   received

medical   treatment   during   filming   and   complained   about   the   cold   conditions during   freezing   UK

weather. The huge prize attracted applications from 81,000 people from around the globe which

were finally cut down to 456 apparently normal people — unlike the similar Netflix reality competition

show   Physical: 100 , which gathered a hundred South Korean competitors at peak fitness, including

national athletes and bodybuilders.   Squid Game: The Challenge   players ranged from a mother and

son to the oldest contestant at the age of 69. "We were interested in the games as a test of human

nature, so we wanted the widest possible variety of people", explained one of the show's executive

producers, John Hay.

When the players were not participating in original games from the series like intricately

cutting out umbrella - shaped honeycomb wafers with a needle, they were fully immersed in the

Squid Game universe — no phones, confined to a prison - style dormitory, sleeping on five - bed - high

bunk beds, and surviving on rationed food served by threatening guards. The Big Brother - like living

space also provided ample time for alliances to form. In this new edition, players were also given

chances to eliminate others, as part of the producers' aim to create drama and reveal players'

characters between games.

The drama's creator Hwang Dong - hyuk had previously said the story "was an allegory or

fable about modern capitalist society". In the original drama, Netflix's most watched serie s,   South

Korean contestants with many debts competed in a series of children's games for a huge cash prize

won only by the last surviving player. Executive producer Hay points out that this show is flipped so

it's driven by opportunity rather than need. "We are giving them the most enormous opportunity

and it turns out that's as strong as a motivation and story".
