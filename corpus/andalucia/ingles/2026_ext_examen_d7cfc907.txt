COMPREHENSION (4 points). READ THE TEXT AND ANSWER ALL THE QUESTIONS.
RENT A GRANDMA

 1          According to Japan’s Ministry of Internal Affairs, around 9.1 million people aged 65 or older are still working. That’s
 2     approximately one in four seniors—many seeking extra income to supplement modest pensions. For older women, the challenge
 3     is especially demanding: many never had access to higher education or long-term employment and now struggle to find meaningful
 4     work later in life. To address this, a Tokyo-based company has created a unique initiative called OK Obaachan, which translates
 5     to ‘OK Granny.’ The project allows people to “rent” a grandmother for an hourly fee, offering both practical help and companionship
 6     while giving older women renewed purpose.
 7          The OK Obaachan program is run by Client Partners, a firm dedicated to connecting women of all ages and backgrounds with
 8     job opportunities. Describing itself as a “women-only company,” it manages around 100 women aged 60 to 94 in the OK Obaachan
 9     division. No degree or professional experience is required—only the wisdom and empathy gained through a lifetime of experience.
10     “Many people want to continue contributing to society as they get older,” says the company. “And the experience and abilities of
11     older people are of great use to society.”
12          At a fixed rate of 3,300 yen per hour (about €19), clients can hire a grandma for a wide variety of tasks: cooking lessons,
13     babysitting, household chores, or simply having someone to talk to. Others reach out for advice on relationships, family conflicts,
14     or even heartbreak. The OK Obaachan website lists a range of stories: grandmas who helped mediate family disputes, taught new
15     mothers how to care for babies, attended weddings in place of missing relatives, or cheered for children at school events.
16          Not everyone can join the program. The ideal OK granny, according to Client Partners, is kind, dependable, and hardworking,
17     with an open mind. That last quality is key, as some clients seek help in situations such as single motherhood or divorce, which
18     conservative Japanese society still views critically.
19          Beyond its novelty, OK Obaachan touches on a deeper social issue. In a society where isolation is rising and family ties are
20     weakening, many older women feel left behind. Combined with sexism often masked as tradition and persistent gender
21     expectations, that sense of invisibility can be profound. For these women, the project offers more than extra income—it restores a
22     sense of ikigai, or “reason for being.”
23          As Japan ages, initiatives like OK Obaachan remind society that experience, kindness, and connection remain invaluable—no
24     matter how old you are.
