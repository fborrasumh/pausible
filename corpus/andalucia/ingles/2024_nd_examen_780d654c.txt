TEXT 1: ICELAND ELVES: REAL OR IMAGINARY?

 1          Iceland is filled with legends and all sorts of mythical creatures. None of them are better known than Iceland elves. They are
 2      present everywhere in the local culture. In Iceland, you leave food outside for the elves during Christmas. You also keep the house

 4      church service. In addition, the elves are on their annual moving day on New Year’s Eve, so people light candles to help them on
 5      their way to their new residence.
 6          Mentions of Icelandic elves can be found in Viking poems dating back to 1,000 years ago. Today elves are believed to be real
 7      by 62% of Icelanders, according to official studies by the University of Iceland. You only have to stop to have a chat with any
 8      Iceland road worker to hear many “true” stories. One of these legendary testimonies comes straight out of the 1930s. Attempts
 9      were made to build a road through a rock known as Elf Hill in the south of Kopavogur. Despite numerous efforts, all attempts were
10      pointless due to unexplained occurrences. The project had to be abandoned and workers have refused to go anywhere near the
11      site ever since. Also, in 2013 Iceland’s supreme court stopped a highway project near Reykjavik because it would disturb a
12      protected area where there was an elf “church”. Roadwork could only start again once the church was safely relocated.
13          Elves have even inspired an actual ecological movement in Iceland. There are organizations and protesters actively
14      campaigning against the development of certain areas of the island because they are supposedly inhabited by elves.

TEXT 2: PLEASE DON’T STOP THE MUSIC

 1         As a child, Steve Howgego loved to sing. His favourite teacher at primary school told him he did it beautifully. He once
 2     overheard his father say: “We should send him to Durham Chorister School.” His mother said he sang awfully and didn’t let him
 3     go. It took Steve years to find his true voice. Now, at 66, Steve makes a living off music despite having received no music education
 4     as a kid.
 5         In his youth, Steve dropped out of his degree course and got a job as a trainee computer programmer. He got promoted and
 6     moved to France, where he worked for Citicorp and bought himself some singing lessons. But he eventually lost interest in
 7     programming and quit.
 8         Back in London, someone suggested Howgego should try gospel singing. At 33, he joined London Community Gospel Choir.
 9     Singing with other singers helped him boost his confidence and vocal ability. But when his sister, to whom he was close, died at
10     the start of the century, Howgego had a breakdown. His voice left him for five years.
11         His second turning point came during therapy sessions, where he made a friend who also sang. His friend suggested Steve

14     reaffirmed him and opened up other possibilities. “What I have found by trusting in my abilities is that one positive thing leads to
15     another,” Steve says. At present, he collaborates with other creatives and is working on a play about his experiences.
