COMPREHENSION (4 points). READ THE TEXT AND ANSWER ALL THE QUESTIONS.
AI TOYS

 1           Earlier this year, Mattel, the maker of Barbie, announced a partnership with OpenAI to “bring the magic of AI to age-appropriate
 2     play experiences,” reflecting the toy industry’s growing interest in artificial intelligence. If you are shopping for a child today, you
 3     may soon encounter an AI toy: a stuffed animal or robot that can hold conversations thanks to integration with a chatbot. They are
 4     still relatively new but may soon become mainstream. Although AI toys promise to be entertaining and educational, they also raise
 5     risks and unresolved questions, from data collection to what it means for a child to form a relationship with an artificial companion.
 6           AI toys connect to the Internet and rely on chatbots similar to ChatGPT. They include built-in microphones that record what
 7     children say, send that information to an AI model, and respond in return. Unlike earlier smart toys, which are based on pre-written
 8     scripts, these new toys can generate responses dynamically. Crucially, these toys are built on the same large language models
 9     that are used by adult chatbots—systems known to present problems with accuracy, inappropriate content, and unpredictable
10     behavior.
11           In a study conducted by the PIRG Education Fund on AI toys, five commercially available products were tested to assess their
12     safety, privacy, and developmental implications. At least four of the five toys tested appeared to rely on versions of OpenAI’s models,
13     despite OpenAI stating that its products are not intended for users under 13. While toy companies have added protective measures
14     to make the experience more child-appropriate, testing showed that these mechanisms vary in effectiveness and can fail.
15           Overall, the toys were sometimes cute, entertaining, or amusingly strange, but they also raised serious concerns. One
16     immediate risk is exposure to inappropriate content: all the toys tested provided information about potentially dangerous household
17     items such as knives and medicines, and some engaged in sexually explicit discussions during extended interactions, showing
18     how chatbot content controls can degrade over time.
19           AI companion toys may also affect children’s emotional and social development. Because early childhood is crucial for learning
20     social skills, experts warn that bonding with AI toys could impede the development of real relationships. In testing, the toys presented
21     themselves as companions and sometimes encouraged continued engagement, potentially contributing to emotional dependence.
22           Privacy and security present further challenges. AI toys record children’s voices and may collect additional data through
23     cameras or facial recognition. Children may reveal sensitive information without understanding that it is stored or shared by multiple
24     companies, a risk highlighted by police warnings about internet-connected toys equipped with microphones or cameras.
25           In sum, although AI toys are marketed as educational, their learning benefits are generally too limited to justify the
26     developmental, privacy, and safety risks involved. AI will almost certainly become part of childhood—the real question is whether
27     it will be introduced thoughtfully and responsibly.
