COMPREHENSION (4 points). READ THE TEXT AND ANSWER ALL THE QUESTIONS.
SPOGOMI

 1         SpoGomi is a combination of two words, sport and gomi, the Japanese word for rubbish. Invented in 2008, SpoGomi was
 2     designed as a competitive activity to encourage people to clean up public spaces. It has since been introduced in Japanese
 3     schools, where students now collect litter while having fun.
 4         According to the rules, teams of three players have 60 minutes to collect as much litter as possible from a designated area.
 5     The waste must be sorted into color-coded bags according to type, such as burnable waste, recyclable plastic, metal cans, and
 6     more. At the end of the competition, the collected litter is weighed and checked for correct sorting, and the team with the highest
 7     score wins. In the event of a tie, the winner is determined by the “quality” of the rubbish collected, with cigarette butts earning the
 8     most points.
 9         The public-spirited sport of SpoGomi quickly gained popularity in Britain. In a surprising outcome, the British team beat the
10     Japanese in the Spogomi World Cup celebrated in Tokyo in 2023. One of the winning competitors was Sarah Parry, a 29-year-old
11     doctor from Glasgow, who—together with her two teammates—collected 28 kilograms more litter than the host nation.
12         Parry describes herself as highly competitive. After her brother came across an advertisement for SpoGomi, she assembled a
13     team and signed up for the World Cup. “We were not avid litter-pickers in our free time,” she explains. “It was luck, competitiveness
14     and enthusiasm more than anything else.”
15         Although litter-picking may sound calm, Parry emphasizes that it is physically demanding. “It’s very difficult,” she says. “I have
16     run 33 marathons, and the sorest my legs have ever been was after winning the SpoGomi World Cup.” She explains that
17     competitors must walk quickly while carrying a lot of awkwardly shaped items, sometimes collecting more than 50 kilograms of
18     waste in a busy urban environment. This involves using very different muscle groups.
19         Parry believes SpoGomi is unlike any other sport: “It is very fun. You don’t often get the chance to play sport in a busy urban
20     area where people don’t understand what you are doing and why you are so excited about spotting a glass bottle.” More importantly,
21     she adds, SpoGomi has drawn attention to a very important issue: the litter problem affecting Britain’s streets. Although the sport
22     originated in Japan, the country has far less litter than the UK. Parry hopes SpoGomi will become massively popular in her country
23     and inspire people to care more about waste. “I wasn’t particularly eco-minded myself, and SpoGomi completely changed my
24     perspective,” she notes.
25         SpoGomi is not really about rewards. Instead, participants of all ages enjoy the competitive nature of the sport, the teamwork
26     that it encourages, the valuable time spent outdoors and, last but certainly not least, the opportunity to keep their cities clean.
27     “That’s what’s really nice about the sport,” Parry concludes.

USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each). CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER ALL THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT
OPTION ONLY.

OPTION 1
REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY
PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.
    o BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.
    o THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.

B1. There were more female players than male players at the game.
There weren’t… There weren’t as many male players as female ones/players at the game.

B2. You shouldn’t post negative comments on social media.
If I… If I were you, I would not post negative comments on social media.

B3. I have such loyal friends that they will never say no to any of my plans.
(SO) My friends are so loyal (that) they will never say no to any of my plans.

B4. It’s possible that I forgot my keys at home.
(MAY) (It’s possible that) I may have forgotten my keys at home. / (It’s possible that) My keys may be at home.

B5. Samantha borrowed Jack’s laptop.
(LENT) Jack lent his laptop to Samantha. / Jack lent Samantha his laptop.

B6. Do teenagers wear baggy jeans these days?
(WORN) Are baggy jeans worn by teenagers these days?
