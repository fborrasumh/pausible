Read the following text:
A recent study has found that intelligent people are more likely to swear. It’s the news that every foul-
mouthed person has been waiting for—the opportunity to excuse your unclean mouth as one of the
side-effects of being smart.
Researchers at the University of Rochester quizzed 1,000 people about 400 typical behaviours, and
discovered a strong link between intelligence and swearing. Participants with higher intellects were
found to be more likely to curse, eat spicy breakfasts, and walk around the house naked. Interestingly,
they also reported greater creativity, emotional awareness, and a tendency to question rules or
traditions that didn’t make sense to them. These findings suggest that intelligence may correlate not
just with knowledge, but also with a more flexible approach to social norms.
Extroverts, as you’d probably expect, like to drive cars faster, gamble, and tell dirty jokes. Introverts, on
the other hand, were found to engage more in quiet or solitary activities such as reading, reflecting, or
listening to music. The contrast between these groups highlights how personality traits influence the
ways people express themselves, whether through bold behaviour or more restrained habits.
The study explored the complexity surrounding each different personality type, and the array of
behaviours associated with each one. Its findings were published in a journal article where researchers
emphasised that everyday actions can reveal hidden aspects of our psychological makeup. They argued
that small, seemingly trivial choices—like language use or morning routines—can serve as powerful
indicators of cognitive and emotional patterns.
Another study previously found that people who can name the most swear words in a minute tend to
score higher on IQ tests. Rather than being the sign of someone with a limited vocabulary, the study
found that swearing proficiency was a sign of rhetorical strength and verbal creativity, showing that
fluent speakers are capable of adapting language expressively depending on mood and context.
Another benefit of swearing is that it acts as a natural pain relief. Psychologists from the University of
Keele conducted tests in which some participants were asked to swear before either doing an intense
session on an exercise bike or squeezing a device that measures hand grip strength. In both situations,
they found that swearing rudely resulted in significant improvements in performance compared with
uttering ‘neutral’ words. Researchers believe this effect may stem from the body’s natural fight-or-flight
response, which boosts endurance and reduces perceived discomfort.
