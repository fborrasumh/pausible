Read the following text:
Teenagers will soon face new restrictions on beauty filters on TikTok amid growing concerns about self-
esteem and anxiety. In the coming weeks, under-18s will be blocked from using filters that artificially
enlarge eyes, plump lips, or alter skin tone in ways beyond traditional makeup. However, playful filters
like bunny ears or dog noses will remain accessible. The changes aim to reduce the pressure on teens to
achieve "perfect" appearances, which many believe contributes to poor mental health, especially among
girls.
The success of these measures depends on users registering their correct age, which is not always the
case. To address this, TikTok will tighten its age verification processes and launch machine learning tools
by the end of the year to detect users under 13. These efforts could lead to thousands of British children
being removed from the platform. Those mistakenly flagged as underage will have the chance to appeal.
TikTok’s lead on child safety stated that while these measures may inconvenience some users, the
platform’s "safety-first approach" takes priority.
These moves align with the UK’s upcoming Online Safety Act, which mandates stricter social media
regulations to protect children online. Ofcom, the UK’s communications regulator reported that TikTok
removed about 1% of a total UK user base for being underage between June 2022 and March 2023.
Ofcom has also highlighted that the platform’s age verification methods require improvement. By next
summer, social media companies will be legally required to implement “highly effective” age checks for
users under 13.
Other platforms are also making changes. Instagram, among others, has recently launched "teen
accounts" that give parents greater control over their children’s activities, including the ability to block
app usage at night. Critics argue that while these measures are welcome, they are largely driven by the
need to follow regulations rather than a proactive commitment to child safety. Andy Burrows, CEO of
the Molly Rose Foundation, called for TikTok to be transparent about its age-assurance methods. The
NSPCC (National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children) described TikTok’s new safeguards as
a positive step but "just the tip of the iceberg", urging social media platforms to develop more effective
solutions for verifying user ages and protecting children online.
