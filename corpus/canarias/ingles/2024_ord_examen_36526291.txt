                                                            GRUPO A
                                            Britney Spears’ book is an angry warning
     American singer Britney Spears has recently published her autobiography, The Woman in Me, a moving
     story about a woman subjected to close observation and ridicule throughout her life. As an example, when
     she was a teenager, journalists repeatedly asked her questions about her breasts and her sex life. As an
     adult, she was “imprisoned” under a conservatorship* supervised by her father, which took away her most
   5 basic human rights for 13 years. During that time, she was not allowed to see her two sons without
     permission, she could not choose her food or drink and her driving licence was confiscated.
     In her book, Spears tells us how, as soon as she exploded onto the pop charts in 1998, the press refused to
     believe she had any real value as an artist. Since her songs were written for her, she was perceived as a
     product and puppet of the music industry, not as an autonomous person. Spears’ publicists infantilised her.
  10 “No one could seem to think of me as both sexy and talented,” she explained.
     Spears’ memoir also recounts her relationship with Justin Timberlake and how, after a devastating
     abortion, Justin Timberlake left her. A break-up that only increased the appetite for gossip about Spears’
     personal life: the media obsessively chased her. Eventually, the pressure became too much, and in 2007,
     Spears decided to cut off her hair. However, instead of seeing this as an act of strength or rebellion, Spears’
  15 action was used as evidence of instability. Within a year, she had been placed under the conservatorship.
     Nevertheless, Spears’ writing turns particularly angry when discussing her father, whom she describes as
     an alcoholic and a failed businessman, as well as an “irresponsible” and “cold” figure who pushed his
     children too hard and was abusive to their mother.
     In her biography, the superstar emphasises that she continued with her career despite her difficulties. The
  20 singer carefully documents the millions everyone else made thanks to her work while she was given a strict
     budget of $2,000 per week.
     The revealing book tells how the turning point came when a kindly nurse showed her images of fans
     discussing the Free Britney movement. Then, with renewed courage, she hired a new lawyer and was
     finally liberated from the conservatorship system two years ago. But The Woman in Me reveals that there
  25 was no happy ending. Although she is free now, it will take her years to recover from the after-effects.
     What remains, not for the first time, is a cautionary tale about fame and the corrupting influence of money,
     and bad news for fans hoping to hear Spears’ new music. “My music was my life, and the conservatorship
     was deadly for that; it crushed my soul,” she admitted.
       * Conservatorship: A legal order that the court dictates when a person is considered incapable of managing his or her life and
       consequently appoints another person to do it.

                                                      GRUPO B
                               How will throwing soup at the Mona Lisa save the planet?
       Now the environmentalists go against the Mona Lisa: they have gone and thrown soup at the most famous
       artwork in the world because they think their ideas about climate change are more important than ordinary
       people getting to admire da Vinci’s masterpiece.
       Two activists from Riposte Alimentaire, a French environmental activist group similar to British
     5 JustStopOil (JSO), recently took the opportunity to protest at the Louvre. After emptying a bottle of an
       orange liquid onto the Mona Lisa, one of the women protesters was captured on video shouting: “What is
       more important: art or the right to a healthy and sustainable diet?” This provocative act was part of a
       broader campaign by Riposte Alimentaire and JSO to stop approving new fossil fuel projects.
       Thankfully, the painting was not damaged. The Mona Lisa has been behind safety glass since the 1950s,
    10 after a vandal threw acid at it. Over the years, there have been other attacks on the painting, and in 2022
       another climate activist, a young man camouflaged as an elderly woman so he could get close enough to
       the painting, spread cake over the glass while shouting, “Think of the planet!”.
       This latest assault on the Mona Lisa indicates the profound and increasingly violent attitude of the climate
       movement. They think that their extreme ideas about climate change are more important than ordinary
    15 people enjoying the world’s masterpieces. JSO already did something very similar to Van Gogh’s
       Sunflowers in 2022, and the similarity is no coincidence: Riposte Alimentaire and JSO are part of the A22
       Network, a very powerful and rich environmental organisation.
       The climate activists are so sure that the world is about to end that they think almost any action, even if
       destructive, is justified. For instance, Extinction Rebellion, another similar organisation, has gone as far
    20 as to state that “its activism isn’t about the climate.” Climate change is, they say, just one “symptom of a
       toxic system” supposedly defined by white man supremacy, patriarchy, and European colonialism, among
       others.
       When these greens throw things at great works of art, their aim, apparently, is to expose our decadence.
       Surprisingly, some still argue that criticising these actions is not worth it. Of course, this perspective is
    25 naive. Some eco-activists have already damaged precious artworks.
       Significantly, these kids protesting for environmental causes come from privileged families enjoying a
       comfortable life in industrial societies. However, they claim we must put an end to those industrial societies
       where they live luxurious lives. It also seems contradictory that those Louvre activists are protesting
       against eco-unfriendly farming practices while French farmers are taking to the streets of Paris to oppose
    30 the green regulations, which they think make their lives intolerable.
