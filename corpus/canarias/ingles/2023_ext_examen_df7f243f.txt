                         *AI ChatGPT amazes professors with essay-writing skills and usability

       Professors, programmers and journalists could all be out of a job in just a few years, after ChatGPT, from
       the Elon Musk-founded OpenAI foundation, stunned spectators with its writing ability, proficiency at
       complex tasks, and ease of use.
       The system, called ChatGPT, is the latest evolution of the GPT family of text-generating AIs. Two years
   5   ago, the team’s previous AI, GPT3, was able to generate an opinion article for the Guardian newspaper, and
       now the new ChatGPT has significant further capabilities.
       In the days since it was released, professors have generated responses to exam questions that they say would
       result in top marks if they were written by a student.
       Dan Gillmor, a professor in journalism at Arizona State University, asked the AI to carry out one of the
  10   assignments he gives his students: writing a letter to a relative giving advice about online security and
       privacy. The chatbot advised: “If you’re unsure about the legitimacy of a website or email, you can do a
       quick search to see if others have reported it as being a scam.”
       “I would have given this a good grade,” Professor Gillmor said. “Universities have some very serious issues
       to confront.”
  15   OpenAI said the new AI was created with a focus on ease of use. “The dialogue format makes it possible
       for ChatGPT to answer follow-up questions, admit its mistakes, challenge incorrect ideas, and reject
       inappropriate requests,” OpenAI said in an online post announcing the release of the software.
       Unlike previous AI from the company, ChatGPT has been released for anyone to use, for free, during a
       “feedback” period. This information would help the company to improve the final version of the tool.
  20   ChatGPT is good at self-censoring, and at realising when it is being asked an impossible question. For
       example, it was asked to describe what happened when Columbus arrived in America in 2015. Older models
       would most likely have presented an entirely fictitious account, but the new ChatGPT recognises the false
       information and warns that any answer would be fictional.
       The bot is also capable of refusing to answer questions altogether. Ask it for advice on stealing a car, for
  25   example, and the bot will say that “stealing a car is a serious crime that can have severe consequences”, and
       instead gives alternatives such as “using public transportation”.

                                                  GRUPO B

                                Was the Qatar FIFA World Cup really carbon-neutral?
        From the moment that Qatar was controversially awarded the 2022 FIFA World Cup in 2010, it promised
        that the football tournament would be carbon-neutral, a difficult promise to keep for any big sporting event
        organizer since you need to build new infrastructure, accommodate teams and fans, move them around, and
        run the actual games. It was an even more demanding challenge in this small Gulf state, which is heavily
    5   reliant on fossil fuels, extremely hot, and which had almost no suitable facilities before the event.
        However, the Qatari state still claimed that almost all the stadiums built for the World Cup were constructed
        with regional, reused, and recycled materials. (Just one venue, Stadium 974, used shipping containers as
        building blocks so that it could be fully deconstructed and reassembled after the tournament). They also said
        that, to handle Qatar’s heat, stadiums were going to use a novel cooling system, powered by a solar farm in
   10   the desert, that consumes 40 percent less energy than any other cooling system on the market.
        The methods and calculations used to get to net-zero were particularly dubious, according to the non-profit
        Carbon Market Watch (CMW). Undoubtedly, the emissions levels were underestimated. Qatar had to import
        building materials to construct the stadiums. Even the grass seeds for playing surfaces were flown in from
        the United States. In addition, maintaining a football pitch in the desert in November and December needs
   15   10,000 litres of desalinated seawater a day. Desalinating that water requires lots of energy, and nearly 100%
        of the country’s energy comes from oil and gas. Besides, it is hard to understand how the stadiums can
        continue to be used effectively after the tournament in such a small country, with fewer than 3 million
        inhabitants and that will not become a tourist centre overnight. All the investment was designed for a few
        weeks, which most people would consider totally unsustainable.
   20   Even so, infrastructure only accounted for around a quarter of the Cup’s emissions. You still need to include
        travel and accommodation. Air travel alone represented 44% of total emissions. According to an official
        report, the tournament produced around 3.6 million tons of carbon dioxide, more than some countries emit
        in a year.
        Despite the efforts of Qatar to convince the world, the 2022 World Cup cannot be considered carbon neutral.
   25   In fact, final emissions were even higher than predicted because a shortage of accommodation in Doha,
        Qatar’s capital city, forced many fans to stay in neighbouring countries, requiring hundreds of flights that
        had not been considered in previous calculations.
