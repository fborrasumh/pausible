                                                       GRUPO A
                         Is it time to rethink the polar bear as a symbol of the climate crisis?
       Alone in the Arctic and surrounded by disappearing sea ice, the polar bear’s dramatic image has become
       a symbol of the climate crisis. Few stories of the climate crisis are better known than the unfortunate
       situation of the polar bear.
       These marine mammals are heavily dependent on sea ice for hunting, and scientists affirm they will
   5   become extinct across much of the region as the Arctic heats. Some scientists warn that many
       subpopulations could disappear entirely this century. In fact, long-term declines have already been
       recorded in three of the 19 polar bear subpopulations found across the Arctic.
       The Norwegian archipelago of Svalbard is the Earth’s most rapidly warming part, where temperatures
       have risen 4ºC on average in the past 50 years and a massive amount of sea ice has disappeared, raising
  10   fears for the survival of 300 bears. Despite this significant change in conditions, the polar bear population
       on Svalbard has not experienced a decline yet. “Biology and ecology are very complicated. I have to say
       that I’m a bit surprised that polar bears do so well in Svalbard because the changes have been so big. They
       have three to four months less sea ice now than three decades ago on average,” says Professor Jon Aars,
       the scientist in charge of the polar bear program at the Norwegian Polar Institute.
  15   On the other hand, Prof. Andrew Derocher, a polar bear expert at the Canadian University of Alberta,
       claims that there are areas of polar bear distribution that are going to be quite robust to the effects of climate
       change. Although he is pessimistic about the future for many subpopulations, including the bears in
       Svalbard, he says we cannot talk about a global state of the polar bears.
       That is why some researchers claim that this accidental icon of the climate crisis is unhelpful and not
  20   universally true so far, and that can obstruct conservation efforts by driving mistrust among some
       Indigenous communities in the Arctic. For example, in the Canadian context, the polar bears, as an icon
       of climate change, have caused many problems. “We used to have a good relationship with Inuit hunters.
       A lot of the hunters that I know think that these mammals will do OK with climate change, and that has
       created some tensions,” Prof. Derocher complains.
  25   Many groups of the world’s 26,000 wild polar bears could disappear entirely this century. However, Prof.
       Derocher asserts that as there are 19 polar bear populations across the Arctic, 19 different scenarios are
       playing out over time. “I think our best analysis is that not all polar bears are going extinct this century,”
       he explains.

                                                     GRUPO B
                                        Who should stop, Mona or her mum?
       Mona likes to be unconventional. She has purple hair one week, green the next. When she was 14, she
       gave herself about 10 ear piercings, and she fills them all with tiny earrings now. Her mother, Kate, noticed
       the first face piercing at 16 immediately – it was a small ring through her nose. As she thought Mona was
       too young, she told her repeatedly to take it out.
   5   Kate’s rule was “You can do what you want when you move out, but while you’re in my house, I’d like
       you to give up piercings and tattoos.” Kate considers that it is out of respect, and she doesn’t really like
       Mona’s tattoos because they are permanent.
       Kate was quite upset with the last noticeable tattoo Mona got on her inner arm, so she told Mona: “While
       you’re under my roof, I don’t want to see any more.” Mona said that was fine, but she went to university,
  10   and every time she came back for the holidays, there was another tattoo or piercing.
       Although Kate thinks that some of the tattoos are quite pretty, she is concerned about how Mona will be
       perceived when applying for jobs. Kate worries about what it will look like when she’s older, but Mona
       just laughs at her. In Kate’s words, “she needs to focus on getting serious employment as she’s just
       interning at a design agency now.”
  15   To Mona, her tattoos and piercings are an extension of herself, as well as an expression of her personality
       and individuality, so they are important to her. When her mum criticises how she looks or tells her not to
       get any more “body modifications”, Mona takes it personally: “Part of me wants to follow my mother’s
       rules just to make sure we don’t argue, but on the other hand I think I am an adult. It’s my body.”
       Mona considers that telling her what to do with her body is a direct attack on her personal rights: “It’s
  20   negating my autonomy just because we live under the same roof. What right does my mum have to tell me
       not to get another tattoo or piercing?”
       Mona has 12 tattoos so far and she will undoubtedly add to that, but for now, she wants an easy life, so
       she guesses things are on pause. But that won’t last forever, and Mona adds: “I don’t have to obey those
       old-fashioned and arbitrary rules just to keep my mum happy. I love her, but she can’t tell me what to do.
  25   I think it’s important for everyone to present themselves as they want. If you’re not harming anyone, who
       cares whether your hair is blue or if you’re covered in metal?”
